Digitalisation does not mean buying new software. It means doing work you now do by hand, often more than once, a single time and reliably. This guide is for owners and managers of smaller businesses who feel they could work better but do not know where to start.

## The short answer

Start with one task that repeats every week and takes the most time. Write down how it works today, remove the steps that add nothing, and only then choose a tool. Aim to see the first result within weeks, not months.

## 1. Find where you lose time

For one week, note every task where you feel you are doing the same thing twice. Look for four patterns in particular:

- **Retyping**: copying data from a message, paper or email into somewhere else.
- **Searching**: spending time looking for the latest version of a file, a price or an order.
- **Waiting**: work stops because someone has to approve or send something.
- **Fixing**: the same mistakes repeat because there is no shared procedure.

A simple table is enough:

| Task | Times per week | Approximate time | Who does it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Collecting orders from locations | 2 | 3 hours | manager |
| Preparing a quote | 5 | 1 hour | owner |
| Looking up customer details | daily | 30 minutes | everyone |

After a week you will have a clear picture of where the time goes. It is often somewhere other than you expected.

## 2. Choose one task

Do not start with everything at once. Choose a task that is:

- **frequent**, so the improvement pays off many times,
- **simple enough** to change within a few weeks,
- **visible**, so the team notices the difference quickly,
- **low risk**, so a mistake during roll-out does not stop the business.

Good first candidates are ordering supplies, handling enquiries, time tracking, travel expense forms and preparing quotes.

## 3. Describe how it works today

Write the task down step by step: who starts it, what they do, with which tool, who they hand it to and where it gets stuck. Do this with the people who actually do the work. Managers often describe how the work should happen; employees know how it really happens.

This step alone often shows that part of the problem lies in the procedure, not the tool.

## 4. Simplify first, then digitalise

Before you move anything into a new tool, remove unnecessary steps. Standardise forms, decide who makes which decision and agree where the one valid version of the data lives.

Digitalise a confusing procedure and you get faster confusion. Digitalise a clear procedure and you get savings.

## 5. Choose a tool you will actually use

First check what you already have. Many businesses pay for Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace and use only email. Forms, shared spreadsheets and simple automation are often already included.

When choosing a new tool, ask four questions:

1. Will everyone who has to use it understand it, including people who avoid computers?
2. Where is the data stored, and does the provider comply with GDPR?
3. Can you export your data at any time if you switch tools?
4. What will the tool cost in three years, when you have more users?

## Common mistakes

- **Buying a tool before understanding the procedure.** The tool then dictates how you work instead of helping you.
- **Too many changes at once.** The team resists because nobody knows what applies any more.
- **A procedure without an owner.** If nobody is responsible, everything slips back within a few weeks.
- **No training.** Even a good tool stays unused if people do not see how it helps them.

## How to know it worked

Before the change, write down three numbers: how long the task takes, how often mistakes happen and how quickly a customer gets an answer. Measure them again a month later. If the numbers have not moved, it is time for a conversation, not a new tool.

Digitalisation is a sequence of small, considered steps. Once the first one delivers, the second is easier.
